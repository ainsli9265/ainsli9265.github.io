For this lab I implemented an interactive cellular automata (like Conway's Game of Life) that turns changes in a 10x10 grid into sound. Each cell of the grid can be alive or dead, with the color indicating the cell age. Each column of the grid is assigned a different musical pitch and certain rows are assigned to different octave multipliers, so a cell's position determines the frequency of the note its sine wave oscillator plays. The automata evolves over time according to the selected birth and survival rules.

The rules are described by how many neighbors a dead cell needs to come alive, and how many neighbors an alive cell needs to survive. For example, the rule "born 3,6 / survive 2,3" means a dead cell needs to have either 3 or 6 alive neighbors to come alive in the next cycle, and an alive cell needs either 2 or 3 alive neighbors to stay alive.

Since its possible for many notes to play at once, I struggled a little bit with clipping and getting the audio to sound nice. I ended up passing all nodes through a compressor before the audio destination, which helps even out the volume difference between 1 or 2 notes playing vs 80. It was also interesting to allow the user to choose between multiple rulesets. I had to find a way to extract the relevant numbers from a selection id, which is why I made a parserule() function to change the text into arrays of neighbor numbers.
